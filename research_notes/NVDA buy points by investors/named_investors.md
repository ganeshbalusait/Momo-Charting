## Stated price, valuation or condition for buying, adding or selling

### Takeaway
Very few named investors give a hard price. The conditions they state are qualitative. Druckenmiller would buy back "if the price came down" or the valuation became reasonable (said in 2024, still regretful in 2026). Burry (Nov 2026) sees a short-term trade but no long-term hold. Wood keeps buying dips but cut her 5-year expected return to 25%. Smith will not buy until the business is predictable.

## Notable bears and what would change their minds

### Takeaway
Burry is the main named bear. He was short via puts and stock in 2025 to mid-2026, covered in the autumn, and now calls NVDA a short-term trade but not a long-term hold. His stated worry is that the moat erodes and that shareholder returns are poor relative to the cash NVDA generates. Soros holds puts, but they look like a hedge on a long position.

### Cited Findings
- **Burry, Nov 2025:** disclosed bearish puts on NVDA and PLTR — [Fortune, 4 Nov 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/11/04/big-short-investor-michael-burry-nvidia-palantir-puts-alex-karp/)

- **Burry, about 1 Oct 2026:** covered all short common-stock positions, NVDA included, and replaced the NVDA short with Sept-2027 puts (as reported) — [Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/michael-burry-doubles-down-on-nasdaq-100-puts-adds-to-semiconductor-shorts-as-nvidia-faces-scrutiny/cNGCfLLR9O4)

### Inferences
- What has moved Burry so far is price. He turned from short to "trade" after the about 30% drawdown from $243 to about $169, which suggests sentiment washout ("shocked at how the market has turned") is his trigger. A long-term buy would apparently need evidence that the moat is holding beyond five years and/or bigger shareholder distributions. This is my reading of his stated objections, not his words.
- The sources conflict on whether Burry is currently net short (puts) or holds calls. As of 20 Nov 2026 his exact position is not verifiable.
