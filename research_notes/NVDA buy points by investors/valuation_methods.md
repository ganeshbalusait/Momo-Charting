## Rule #1 / Phil Town sticker price

### Takeaway
No public source found computes a Rule #1 sticker price for NVDA. Only the method is published.

### Cited Findings
- Sticker price is the most you can pay and still earn a 15% minimum acceptable rate of return. The margin-of-safety buy price is 50% of sticker, and Rule #1 says to sell at sticker. — [Rule One Investing](https://www.ruleoneinvesting.com/blog/stock-market-basics/calculate-the-buy-price-of-a-stock/); [Rule One sticker guide](https://www.ruleoneinvesting.com/blog/how-to-invest/understanding-sticker-price-a-guide-to-calculating-a-companys-true-value)

### Inferences
- Illustrative calculation only, not a published figure. Assumptions: EPS ~$9 TTM (my estimate), growth 25% (same cap as Lynch), future P/E 2x growth = 50, 10 years. That gives future EPS ~$84 and a future price ~$4,190, discounted at 15% for 10 years to a sticker of ~$1,035 and a MOS price of ~$518. The result is extremely sensitive to the growth input. Using Town's usual cap of the lower of analyst growth and historical growth, with a P/E cap, it would be much lower. Treat this as a demonstration of how sensitive the method is, not a value.

## What the methods disagree on

### Takeaway
The spread runs from ~$21 (Graham) to ~$388 (GF Value). It is driven by three assumptions: (1) whether FY27 earnings are a cyclical peak or a base, (2) the long-run growth rate and how it is capped, and (3) whether the model looks backward (history-based multiples and FCF) or forward (analyst forecasts).

### Cited Findings
- Morningstar treats FY27 (~80-99% growth) as the peak, with margins easing from mid-70s to high-60s. It still values the stock at ~20x FY28 earnings at $280 and higher at $310. — [Morningstar AU](https://www.morningstar.com.au/stocks/this-onetime-ai-market-darling-is-now-surprisingly-undervalued)
- The Morningstar bear case ($180) covers custom XPUs and an AI capex return-on-investment downturn. Customer concentration and China export limits are also named risks. — same source
- Lynch-style models cap growth at 25% even though trailing 5-yr growth was 172%. — [valueinvesting.io](https://valueinvesting.io/NVDA/valuation/fair-value)
- GuruFocus calls NVDA a "Possible Value Trap" even while its GF Value is 41% above price. — [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9040619/nvda-looks-413-undervalued-on-gf-value-at-22501) (snippet)
- The Q4 FY27 gross-margin guide of 71-72%, down from 74.9% in Q3, is the first visible step in the margin easing Morningstar models. — [NVIDIA press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)

### Inferences
- History-based models are skewed by NVDA's small, pre-AI past. Graham Number and projected-FCF use thin historical book value and FCF, so they give very low values. GF Value's historical multiples were set during hyper-growth, so it gives high values.
- Forward DCFs (Morningstar, SWS, Alpha Spread) differ mainly on how fast growth fades after FY27-28 and on terminal margin. Discount rates are not disclosed in the sources found.
- Nine-month GAAP EPS ($7.55) is above non-GAAP ($6.77). This suggests non-operating gains (likely investment gains, unverified), which inflate GAAP-based P/E and Lynch inputs. Earnings-based methods should use non-GAAP or run-rate EPS.
