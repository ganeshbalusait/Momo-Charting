# Copy Tesla's data loop, not its brain

Tesla FSD and the leading humanoid robots are not one giant AI that "decides". Each is a stack of layers. A slow, broad model understands the situation and sets an intent. A fast, narrow policy acts on fresh sensor data many times a second. A hard reflex and safety layer sits underneath, and nothing above it can override it. Around all of this runs a **data engine**: cheap triggers flag failures and human takeovers, those moments are labelled in hindsight, retrained on, and released in stages. That loop is what makes them improve, and it works only because a road gives billions of plentiful, stable labels. Markets give none of those three things. They change regime, they adapt against you, and no simulator reacts like a real order book. LLM trading agents tested honestly (no data leakage, longer windows, real money) show edges that are small, unstable or absent. Professional firms use LLMs upstream, as research assistants, with humans and fixed statistical gates in front of anything that trades. So AGX should copy the robot *discipline* (data engine, shadow mode, regression tests, staged rollout, a kill switch nothing can override) and not the "let one big model drive" idea. AGX's own truth check is already the FSD lesson in miniature. The BOT's mid-price score is an open-loop test, and real fills disagree with it: winners filled 26% of the time and losers 37%. The first job is to turn that gap into a trustworthy "world model" of fills. Only then does any AI deserve more authority.

A note on sources. Research claims are cited inline. Facts about AGX come from Ganesh's own session and are marked **(AGX app fact)**. Mappings and gate numbers are this report's analysis unless a citation says otherwise. Most robot and Tesla numbers are company claims that nobody outside has checked.

## FSD and humanoids run three speeds plus a learning loop

**Tesla FSD** began as a modular pipeline: neural-network perception fed a planner of roughly **300,000 lines of hand-written C++** ([Analytics India Mag](https://analyticsindiamag.com/the-brilliant-language-of-lanes); secondary, no primary transcript). From v12 (employees in Nov 2023) one network trained on human driving video goes straight from camera pixels, route and speed to the driving output ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/1713/tesla-fsd-v12-rolls-out-to-employees-with-update-2023-38-10)). Tesla's AI chief says this was the only way to handle "mini-trolley problems" such as whether to drive through a big puddle, which are too fuzzy to hard-code ([Humanoids Daily](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/tesla-ai-chief-details-unified-world-simulator-for-fsd-and-optimus)). By v14.3 a **reinforcement-learning stage** follows the imitation training, and one model serves FSD, Summon and Robotaxi ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/cs/software-updates/version/2026.20.6.11/release-notes); [Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/de/software-updates/version/2026.2.9.8/versionshinweise)).

The engine behind it matters more than the network. In 2021 Karpathy described **221 hand-written triggers** running on the fleet. They flagged interesting or failing moments, and only those clips were uploaded. Big offline networks labelled them, people cleaned up the labels, wrong predictions became fixed regression "unit tests", and the loop ran **seven times** for one release ([Dynamically Typed](https://dynamically-typed.netlify.app/stories/2021/karpathy-autopilot-cvpr)). Tesla's patents describe a small trigger classifier that engineers can retarget remotely as a "campaign" ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/?p=127323)). They also describe labelling a moment using what happened *after* it, which is hindsight labelling ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12223428)). Shadow mode runs a candidate model silently beside the driver and scores it against what really happened ([TMC quoting Tesla's Q2 2019 letter](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/were-hw3-gen-nns-running-passively-since-july.179647/)). Releases go out in stages: internal testers, then employees, then early access, then everyone ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/2118/tesla-releases-fsd-v1243-to-employees)). Robotaxi dropped its human backups one at a time inside a geofence: a safety monitor in the car, then a chase car, then remote operators ([driveteslacanada](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/tesla-begins-public-unsupervised-robotaxi-rides-in-austin/); [TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/tesla-reveals-two-robotaxi-crashes-involving-teleoperators/)). Tesla's own engineer warns that **"loss on open-loop predictions might not correlate to great performance in the real-world"**, which is why Tesla built a learned world simulator for closed-loop testing ([Humanoids Daily](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/tesla-ai-chief-details-unified-world-simulator-for-fsd-and-optimus)).

**Humanoid robots** made the layering explicit. Figure's Helix pairs a **7B-parameter "System 2" model at 7-9 times a second** that understands the scene and instruction with an **80M-parameter "System 1" policy at 200 times a second** that moves the body. The slow layer writes its intent to shared memory, and the fast layer reads the latest value on every tick without waiting ([Figure](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix)). Helix 02 adds a **"System 0" at 1,000 times a second** for balance ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/figure-helix-02-humanoid-robot-autonomy/)). NVIDIA's GR00T N1 uses the same two-speed split ([arXiv 2503.14734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14734)). Physical Intelligence's π0.5 writes its plan as a readable text subtask such as "pick up the plate" before acting ([arXiv 2504.16054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.16054)). Google's Gemini Robotics splits planner and executor into separate models, and the planner can call tools ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/gemini-robotics-15-brings-ai-agents-into-the-physical-world/)).

Two robot ideas matter most for AGX. First, Physical Intelligence's RECAP method trains in three steps: demonstrations, then **expert corrections at the exact moments the robot goes wrong**, then learning from its own runs. A learned **value function** flags a bad grasp early, before the failure shows. PI says this more than doubled throughput on hard tasks ([PI](https://www.pi.website/blog/pistar06); company claim). Second, 1X found that **training loss did not predict real-world success, but a world-model evaluator did**. It replays candidate policies from the same starting states, including states where the live robot failed. By 1X's maths, a 70%-accurate evaluator picks the better of two policies 90% of the time when they truly differ by 15 points ([1X](https://www.1x.tech/discover/redwood-ai-world-model)). The honest status is sobering. Musk admitted in January 2026 that **no Optimus robots were doing useful factory work** ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/01/28/musk-admits-no-optimus-robots-are-doing-useful-work-at-tesla-after-claiming-otherwise/)). The WSJ found 1X's NEO "not autonomous at all" in its demo ([Daring Fireball/WSJ](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2025/10/31/stern-1x-neo)). Figure's biggest real deployment was an 11-month BMW pilot of 1,250+ hours ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/figure-humanoid-robots-retires-bmw)). The robots' credibility problem is curated demos without failure rates. That is the same trap as judging a trading rule on its best trades.

## Five robot ideas break on contact with markets

**End-to-end learning has no fuel in trading.** Tesla claims about **12 billion supervised miles** and a fleet that can supply "500 years of driving data every single day" ([Not a Tesla App](https://notateslaapp.com/news/4481/summary-of-teslas-2026-q2-earnings-call-cybercab-fsd-ai4-and-more); [Humanoids Daily](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/tesla-ai-chief-details-unified-world-simulator-for-fsd-and-optimus)). Every frame comes with a free label: what the driver did next. AGX's truth check produced **105 real fills on Paper 3 and 22 on Paper 5 in about two weeks** (AGX app fact). Each label is a noisy P&L, and the "physics" behind it changes. Finance RL researchers name exactly these two blockers: the market is not stationary, and there is no realistic simulator ([arXiv 2003.10014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.10014)). Even XTX, with **20,000 GPUs and 390 PB of storage**, describes its work as price forecasting, not autonomy ([Kyodo/XTX](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202402226932)).

**Sim-to-real fails because simulated markets do not fight back.** Many order-book simulators fail to reproduce basic patterns of real markets ([arXiv 1912.04941](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1912.04941)). Simple backtests overstate results because they leave out adverse fills ([arXiv 2409.12721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.12721)). A road does not change its behaviour because you drove on it. A published market edge loses about **26% of its return out-of-sample and 58% after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff](https://typeset.io/papers/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-3rinmhqstl)). Later data puts the decay at up to 72% ([JFQA](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225/S0022109022000874a.pdf/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies.pdf)).

**A shadow trade is not comparable to a shadow steer, and AGX has proved it.** In FSD, "the model would have steered left" can be checked directly against what the driver did. In options, "the BOT would have bought at mid" says nothing about whether anyone would have sold to you there. Limit orders suffer a winner's curse. They fill more often when the price is about to move against you ([Hollifield, Miller & Sandås](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/Dp2843.pdf)), and real fills diverge from what quote snapshots suggest, more so in fast, thin markets ([Albers et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v25y2025i6p919-947.html)). AGX's truth check found exactly this (AGX app facts):

- BOT winners filled **26%** of the time and losers **37%**.
- Missed orders had a median spread of **34%**, against **17%** for filled ones.
- Orders went out about **230 seconds** late.
- Real ROI on filled trades came in below the BOT's claim in both accounts: Paper 3 −10.7% against the BOT's −6.3%, and Paper 5 +11.6% against the BOT's +27.9%.

Retail weekly options average a **12.6% bid-ask spread**, and retail traders in them lose money on average ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya, JF 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/17688.html)). Retail options traders lose about **5-9% around earnings**, and 10-14% when big moves are expected ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/losing-optional-retail-option-trading-expected-announcement)). The starting odds for AGX's niche are negative after costs.

**Continuous learning from live P&L chases noise.** A robot that drops a cup gets an unambiguous label. A momentum call that lost money may have been a good trade on a bad day. In 888 Quantopian algorithms, the backtest Sharpe ratio explained only **about 1-2% of the variation in live Sharpe**, and authors who ran more backtests had bigger gaps ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=28573)). AGX's own record agrees (AGX app facts): the Jev score is near a coin flip (AUC 0.53), the edge search found no lasting entry rule, the "good trade" model predicted volatility rather than direction, and the shadow filter scored AUC 0.52-0.56. Retraining nightly on recent winners would teach the system the last two weeks of luck.

**"Imitate the expert" copies the expert's habits.** FSD learned human rolling stops and was recalled over traffic-law behaviour ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/02/16/tesla-recalls-all-362758-vehicles-with-full-self-driving-beta-may-cause-crashes-ota-software-update-elon-musk/)). Imitating a trader's past trades would copy his losing habits along with his good ones. Overrides are still valuable, but only when they are scored by outcome, not copied blindly.

Some things do transfer cleanly: shadow mode (as long as fills are priced realistically), hard safety limits, logs of every decision, takeover-as-data, regression replays of past failure days, and staged rollout. The finance notes reach the same split independently ([arXiv 2411.12746](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.12746)).

## Real firms keep LLMs upstream and humans in front of orders

**No primary source found shows a professional firm letting an LLM place orders.** Man Group's AlphaGPT is "a digital three-person research team". An idea agent proposes hypotheses, an implementer writes the code, and an evaluator runs significance and risk tests. AI-generated signals must clear **the same thresholds as human research**, an Investment Committee reviews them, every step is logged, and Man says it "can't leave it unsupervised just yet" ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha)). Man names its biggest risk as **multiple testing**: fast testing of many variants turns up lucky rules that fail live ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha)). Citadel's Ken Griffin says generative AI helps productivity but "for uncovering alpha it just falls short" ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/generative-ai-fails-to-deliver-alpha-for-hedge-funds-says-griffin/)). Where reinforcement learning has a real industrial record, it is in **execution**, meaning how to work an order, not what to buy. JPMorgan's LOXM ran inside the bank's risk framework ([Markets Media](https://marketsmedia.com/j-p-morgan-ai-trading-global/)), and those results are the bank's own claims.

**Academic AI trading agents look good only in tests that flatter them.** TradingAgents reported Sharpe ratios of 5.6-8.2 over a three-month 2024 window on three mega-caps, with no trading costs. The window may fall inside the models' training data ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138v4)). FINSABER re-ran FinMem and FinAgent over 20 years with less bias. **Buy-and-hold beat FinMem at p ≈ 1e-5**, and neither LLM had significant alpha. The agents were "too cautious when risk is rewarded and too aggressive when it is penalised" ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)). StockBench used a window after the models' training cutoff because **GPT-5 could recite AAPL's 2021 trend from memory**. Margins over buy-and-hold were about 2 points over four months, and the agents made arithmetic and output-format errors ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v2)). In real money, Alpha Arena gave six LLMs $10k each in crypto, and **four of six lost about 40-60%** in about 2.5 weeks ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/ai-model-grok-4-2-triumphs-in-trading-tournament/)). The organiser says "it's not yet feasible to directly hand over money to an LLM and let it trade on its own" ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). Agent Market Arena found that **the framework around the model matters more than which LLM is inside** ([arXiv 2510.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11695)). No study found tests LLM agents on options or on intraday momentum, so AGX is off the map and must produce its own evidence.

**Regulators now name AI agents.** FINRA's 2026 oversight report flags "unsupervised execution of regulated actions" by agents. It expects defined human-in-the-loop points, logs of agent actions, and guardrails ([Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2025/12/finras-2026-regulatory-oversight-report-continued)). Knight Capital is the warning case. A bad deployment sent **over 4 million orders in 45 minutes and lost more than $460M**, and **97 warning emails** went unread before the open ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)). The lesson: an alarm should halt trading, not send an email. These rules bind broker-dealers, not a personal app. But AGX has other users, and an AI that trades *other people's* money, or tells them what to buy, moves AGX toward regulated territory. That needs a lawyer's answer before Stage 4 is offered to anyone but Ganesh. This report is not legal advice.

## AGX target design: AI proposes, code trades, a governor can stop everything

The design follows the robot layering but swaps the roles. In robots, the learned policy is the star. In AGX, the evidence says the fast loop must stay deterministic and testable, and the AI's value is in reading, reviewing and proposing. That is how Man uses agents, and it is where LLMs measurably help (reading news and text) ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v2)).

| Agent (layer) | What it does | May | May not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data Health Guard** (System 0 input) | Checks quote age, bar gaps, scanner/TOS parity, Schwab/Alpaca feed status | Mark a symbol or the whole app "unsafe to trade" | Be overridden by any agent |
| **Scanner** (perception, existing) | RVOL, squeeze, Skittles, Setup tags, A+/A grade | Publish signals | Trade, or change its own maths without a regression test |
| **Rule Engine / BOT** (System 1) | ~50 rules turn signals into entries and exits | Request an order through Execution | Size above the Governor's limits, or run a rule not marked promoted |
| **Execution** (System 1, code) | Sends a limit order within seconds of the signal, carrying bid/ask; re-pricing ladder; spread filter | Re-price inside a fixed, logged ladder | Exceed the max spread or max slippage, or chase |
| **Risk Governor** (System 0) | Max premium per trade, open positions, daily loss, orders per minute, allow-list, one-tap kill | Halt all trading instantly; flatten positions on a kill | Be edited by any agent; limits change only by Ganesh's hand |
| **Momentum Watch** (System 2, LLM) | Every few minutes, reads news, market mood and higher timeframes, then writes an "intent card" | Narrow the allowed setups, flag news risk, write plain-English advice | Add trades, widen limits, or send orders. Its numbers are recomputed in code |
| **Trade Progress score** (value function) | Compares a live trade with the typical path for its setup | Raise an early-warning flag | Exit trades until it passes its own gate |
| **Truth Check / Fill Model** (world model) | Joins BOT trades to real paper fills; learns fill odds from spread, delay and next move | Re-price every BOT trade as "fillable ROI" | Be used for promotion until its predictions match paper results |
| **Trigger Recorder** (data engine) | Saves full snapshots on missed winners, big BOT-vs-real gaps, stale quotes and overrides; adds hindsight labels | Write to the research dataset | Touch live settings |
| **Nightly Research Team** (Ollama: planner, rotating specialist, reviewer, critic, Hermes) | The Man Group pattern: idea, then implement, then a critique of closed trades and triggered cases | Propose rules as test files | Promote anything. Every variant it tries is counted |
| **Referee** (code, not AI) | Walk-forward tests, Deflated Sharpe, overfitting probability, comparison with the BOT baseline | Pass or fail a candidate against pre-set numbers | Be bypassed; its thresholds are fixed before the test runs |
| **Promotion Gate** (Ganesh) | Final human sign-off, as Man's committee does | Approve a stage move | Be skipped. Paper 5 auto-promotion moves behind this gate |

The data flows in one direction during market hours, and the learning loop runs only at night:

```
Market hours (fast loop, code only):
  Schwab/Alpaca feeds -> Data Health Guard -> Scanner -> Rule Engine
     -> (filtered by the latest Momentum Watch intent card, which can only narrow)
     -> Risk Governor check -> Execution -> Alpaca paper (later: broker)
  Everything is logged; Trigger Recorder snapshots the interesting moments.

Every few minutes (slow loop, LLM):
  News + market mood + higher timeframes -> Momentum Watch -> intent card
  (the fast loop reads the latest card and never waits for it)

Nightly (learning loop, offline):
  Fills + logs + triggers + Ganesh overrides -> Truth Check / Fill Model
     -> Nightly Research Team proposes rule variants
     -> Referee (walk-forward, Deflated Sharpe, overfitting test, real-fill ROI)
     -> shadow for N days -> Promotion Gate (Ganesh) -> regression suite -> release
```

Paper 5's nightly planner is the part most at odds with the evidence. It proposes, back-tests, forward-tests and promotes a rule automatically (AGX app fact). That is exactly the "multiple-testing machine" Man Group warns about. López de Prado calls the most common error repeating test runs "until a good-looking result appears" ([Oxford Business Law Blog](https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/business-law-blog/blog/2018/03/machine-learning-funds-and-investment-malpractice)). The $500 rollback is a sound System 0 reflex. The *promotion* step needs the Referee and Ganesh in front of it, and it must count every variant the planner has ever tried.

There is one conflict to settle with data rather than opinion. Ganesh's rule is "use only mid order", while the in-progress fix re-prices up to mid + ¼ spread (AGX app facts). The truth check shows mid-only orders are adversely selected: the winners slip away. Under the "no guess work" rule, run both ladders side by side on paper and keep whichever gives the better *real-fill* ROI.

## Four stages, each opened by numbers rather than impressions

The gates below are this report's proposals. The method behind them comes from the notes: Deflated Sharpe and Minimum Track Record Length ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)), probability of backtest overfitting via CSCV ([SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)), the Quantopian finding that backtests barely predict live results, and FSD's metric-fragility lesson. Community tracker numbers for FSD swung between **4,109 and 809 city miles per critical disengagement**, partly because a few drivers dominated the ratio ([Dominik Roth](https://blog.dominik-roth.eu/posts/fsd_14.html)). So each gate pairs an average with a "how many days went badly" count.

A rough guide to sample sizes. This is an illustrative assumption to check against AGX's own data. If one trade's ROI typically varies by about ±100%, the uncertainty on an average falls only with the square root of the number of trades. **22 fills** gives about ±21 points of noise, so Paper 5's +11.6% cannot be told apart from zero. Telling a true +10% average from zero takes roughly **400 real fills**. At Paper 3's pace of about 105 fills in two weeks, that is about two months.

| Stage | What it unlocks | Gate to enter the next stage (all must hold) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Trust the BOT** | BOT numbers shown as "fillable ROI", not mid ROI | Over **≥ 20 trading days and ≥ 300 orders sent**: median signal-to-order delay **≤ 10 s** (from ~230 s). Fill rate **≥ 60%** of eligible signals (from 26-37%). Winners' fill rate within **5 points** of losers' (from 26% vs 37%). On filled trades, the Fill Model's predicted ROI is within **±3 points** of real paper ROI, and the BOT-vs-paper gap shrinks from 4.4 points (Paper 3) and 16.3 points (Paper 5) to that band. Zero trades sent on data the Health Guard marked stale |
| **2. Momentum Watch (advice only)** | The intent card is shown to Ganesh and logged in shadow | Tested only on dates **after the LLM's training cutoff** ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v2)). On **≥ 500 hindsight-labelled signals**, its "skip / allow" advice reaches **AUC ≥ 0.60** out of sample (past AI attempts reached 0.52-0.56). Shadow-filtered fillable ROI beats unfiltered BOT ROI over **≥ 4 walk-forward folds**, each about one week of testing after the earlier weeks. Schema errors **0%** after the code checks. If it fails, it stays as commentary |
| **3. Paper AI trader** | Advice may narrow the BOT's trades automatically on a paper account | **≥ 400 real paper fills over ≥ 40 trading days**, including both up and down market weeks. Real-fill ROI beats the Paper 3 BOT baseline over the same days, with a one-sided t-test **p < 0.05**. **Deflated Sharpe probability ≥ 0.95**, counting every variant the planner tried. **Overfitting probability < 0.2**. Track record at least as long as Minimum Track Record Length. Risk Governor limits respected **100%**. Losing days beyond the limit **≤ 1 in 20**. Ganesh signs off |
| **4. Real money, one tap** | Ganesh's own account only, one contract, with a hard daily loss limit | Each tap confirms the order. The daily loss limit sits in the Governor. Size goes up only after another **≥ 200 real-money fills** keep real ROI within the Stage 3 range. Expect the live edge to come in smaller than paper (decay of about 26% or more ([McLean & Pontiff](https://typeset.io/papers/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-3rinmhqstl))). Other users get advice only, until legal review |

Before any stage moves, every past failure becomes a fixed replay test, as in FSD's regression suite. The AAPL 11-04 case (BOT +232%, unfilled limit at $3.15), the missed-winner days, and the stale-data days must be replayed by each new version, with results no worse than before. Walk-forward testing (train on earlier weeks, test on the next week, then roll forward) is the minimum standard. López de Prado argues a combinatorial cross-validation method beats walk-forward, but that source was not fetched for this report.

## Top risks

The evidence has limits. Nearly all robot and Tesla figures are company claims. No study tests LLM or robot-style agents on short-dated options or intraday momentum. The fill-rate and ROI gate levels above are reasoned starting points, not published standards. They should be re-set once Stage 1 shows how much a single AGX trade really varies.

## Conclusion

The surprise in the robot story is that the famous neural network is the *last* thing to copy. Tesla and Physical Intelligence improved because they built a machine for collecting their own failures: triggers, takeovers, hindsight labels, replay tests and staged releases. The network simply consumed that output. AGX already has the start of that machine. The truth check is a disengagement report, missed winners are the equivalent of takeovers, and the Paper accounts are shadow fleets. What AGX lacks is the step robots took after their first demos: an evaluator whose scores have been shown to match reality. For AGX that evaluator is a fill model built from real paper fills, and until it exists every AI score sits on top of mid-price numbers that were too optimistic in both accounts.

The practical upshot is that AGX's AI should be judged the way 1X judges its evaluator: by whether its predictions line up with real fills, not by how clever its reasoning sounds. If the Momentum Watch can't beat AUC 0.60 after the training cutoff, that is not a failure of the project. It is the "no guess work" rule doing its job, and the AI stays a reader and reviewer while the deterministic scanner and BOT keep the wheel.
