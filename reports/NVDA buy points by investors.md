## The short answer

**Most outside price levels for NVDA sit well above today's price, but the methods that treat NVDA as a cyclical chip stock say "not yet."** Wall Street's average 12-month target fell to **$232.59** on Nov 19, 2026, about 59% above the $145.98 close, after a wave of cuts that followed Jensen Huang's exit as CEO ([TipRanks](https://tipranks.com/stocks/nvda/forecast)). Forward-looking valuation models land between about $200 and $310, so they also call the stock cheap. Your app's two screens are the outliers, and they are the strict end of the range. The Rule #1 screen puts fair value at about $135, close to where NVDA trades now, and would only buy at half that, $67.72. The Lynch screen refuses to give a price at all. That refusal matches what Lynch himself taught about cyclicals and what the company's own numbers show: inventory nearly doubled this year and gross margin is guided down. The famous individual investors give almost no hard prices. They split down the middle in their July-September filings, and none of those filings reflect the November crash. The core disagreement is simple. Bulls see a stock at about **9 times next year's expected earnings** and call it a bargain. Cyclical investors see the same low number as the classic warning sign of a profit peak.

## November 2026 reset every number in this report

Two events in one week made most older price levels stale. Read every figure below with its date in mind.

**The CEO change (what NVIDIA confirmed).** NVIDIA announced that Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately, and named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO while the company searches for a permanent CEO ([iConnect007, NVIDIA announcement](https://iconnect007.com/article/152377/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition-as-jensen-huang-resigns-colette-kress-named-interim-ceo/152374/pcb); [StreetInsider/Reuters](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Nvidia+CEO+Jensen+Huang+steps+down/27332402.html)). NVIDIA gave no reason. According to press reports, the departure followed reported personal-conduct issues and a board review; the company has not confirmed those reports. Reports of the first-day drop range from about -5% at the close to -14% intraday ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-shares-tumble-as-wall-street-reacts-huang-s-exit/)).

**The earnings (fact, company release).** On Nov 17, NVIDIA reported its third quarter of fiscal 2027. NVIDIA's fiscal year ends in late January, so "fiscal 2027" (FY27) runs roughly Feb 2026 to Jan 2027, and FY28 runs to Jan 2028. Revenue was **$115.8B, up 103%** from a year earlier. Data-center revenue was $107.6B. Earnings per share (EPS, profit divided by shares) were $2.70 under standard accounting (GAAP) and $2.68 on the company's adjusted basis. The fourth-quarter guide is **$130B, plus or minus 3%**, with gross margin (the share of each sales dollar left after making the product) guided to 71-72%, down from 74.8% ([NVIDIA press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). There was no stock split in 2026. All prices here are on the same scale as since the June 2024 10-for-1 split.

**The price path (fact, market data).** NVDA set a record close of about **$238.90 on Oct 5, 2026** ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=316268)). Sources disagree on the exact intraday high: one says $243.37 on Oct 6 ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/michael-burry-nvidia-short-term-trade-long-term-warning-1831723)), another about $244. ## Wall Street cut targets by about 30% without cutting earnings

A **price target** is an analyst's guess at where the stock will trade in 12 months. The **consensus target** is the average of all of them. It is an opinion averaged, not a fact.

The cuts came in two waves. In wave one (Nov 11-16), firms reacted to the CEO exit. **Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon** went from Outperform to Market Perform and halved his target from $400 to **$200**. He kept his earnings estimates unchanged and blamed leadership uncertainty, saying the firm "may not yet fully understand how deep the issue goes" ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/bernstein-halves-nvidia-target-to-200-the-3rd-broker-in-2-days-to-step-back-over-huang/)). **Baird** went to Neutral and cut from $500 to **$200**, warning the stock "may not have bottomed" and pointing to the February report as the next chance for answers. **StoneX** moved to Hold and suspended its target. **Goldman Sachs** kept Buy but cut from $300 to **$220**, calling the exit a "meaningful, but measured negative" ([Stocktwits, Nov 12](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/jensen-huang-s-exit-from-nvidia-raises-execution-governance-and-broader-ai-risks-analysts-say-citi-sees-ai-demand-holding-strong/cNGCGxzR9ar)). William Blair, Oppenheimer and Evercore ISI were also reported to downgrade; the Oppenheimer details could not be confirmed.

In wave two (Nov 18-19, after earnings), most Buy-rated firms kept Buy but trimmed targets. The pattern is clear in TipRanks' list: BofA **$280**, Morgan Stanley **$260**, Jefferies and Wells Fargo **$250**, BMO **$240**, JPMorgan **$230** (raised from $215), RBC and Piper Sandler **$230**, Citi **$225**, UBS and Goldman **$220**, TD Cowen **$200**. Evercore ISI holds at **$184** and KeyBanc at Hold with no target ([TipRanks](https://tipranks.com/stocks/nvda/forecast)). Barclays set Overweight at **$210**, saying results showed "this wasn't a shell game with China soaking up excess demand," but that leadership "remains in limbo" ([Stocktwits, Nov 18](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/nvda-premarket-nvidia-leadership-decision-remains-in-limbo/cNbgbVYR9Lg)).

The key point is the **P/E**: price divided by earnings per share. It tells you how many years of profit you pay for. "Forward P/E" uses next year's expected profit. Consensus FY28 EPS was about **$15.75** in mid-November ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/bernstein-halves-nvidia-target-to-200-the-3rd-broker-in-2-days-to-step-back-over-huang/)). On that number, $146 is about **9.3 times** earnings. Post-earnings Buy targets of $220 to $260 equal about 14 to 16.5 times. Bernstein's old $400 was about 25 times. So analysts did not lower their profit forecasts. They lowered the multiple they will pay, because of key-person and governance risk. The next-12-month P/E was already falling before the CEO news: 20.8x at the end of July, 16.9x on Oct 30, 15.4x on Nov 6, against a three-year average of 30.1x ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/nvidias-fiscal-2028-revenue-estimate-is-up-25-since-june-heres-what-november-17-has-to-prove)).

Aggregators disagree. TipRanks' $232.59 average is the freshest. Investing.com snapshots still show averages of $254 to $303, and a TIKR article cites a "Street target" near $329. Those include targets set before the November cuts and are stale ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/equities/nvidia-corp-consensus-estimates)). Even TipRanks is not fully consistent: its monthly table shows a different Buy/Hold count than its headline summary. Who set the $130 low and the single Sell rating could not be identified.

## Valuation methods span $21 to $388, and your app sits at the strict end

A **fair value** is what a method says the stock is worth today, not where it will trade. A **DCF** (discounted cash flow) model adds up all the cash a company is expected to earn in future years and shrinks each year's cash back to today's dollars, because money later is worth less than money now. Small changes in growth assumptions swing the answer a lot. The **PEG ratio** is the P/E divided by the growth rate; Peter Lynch treated a PEG of about 1 as fair. The **Graham Number** is Benjamin Graham's ceiling price for a defensive investor, built only from current earnings and book value (assets minus debts). **Rule #1** (Phil Town's method, which your app uses as its Buffett-style screen) estimates a "sticker price" that would earn 15% a year, then buys only at half of it.

**Morningstar is the clearest case of a stale number.** Its $310 value was set after the August report and repeated in the pre-earnings preview. A Nov 18 report says the analyst, Brian Colello, now uses $250, even while raising his 2027 revenue assumption to $740B. Morningstar's own pages blocked direct reading, so the $250 rests on one secondary report. Either way, even Morningstar's earlier **$180 bear case** sits above today's price.

**The two "Lynch values" disagree for a reason that matters.** The valueinvesting.io formula treats NVDA as a fast grower: fair P/E equals growth, capped at 25, so fair value is 25 times trailing earnings. That number rises automatically with profit. It doubled from $102 in January to $200 in November because trailing EPS doubled. It also looks behind: NVIDIA's nine-month GAAP EPS alone is $7.55 ([NVIDIA press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)), so an updated figure would be higher. Your app's Lynch screen takes the other branch of Lynch's own system. He sorted stocks into types, and for a **cyclical** he said not to use a growth-based fair value at all. That is why your screen gives no price. Both are "Lynch." They answer different questions. Note too that nine-month GAAP EPS ($7.55) is above adjusted EPS ($6.77), which suggests one-off gains are inflating the trailing figure that formula screens use.

**Your Rule #1 screen is the strictest view.** Its $67.72 buy price is exactly half its $135 fair value, in line with the method's 50% margin of safety ([Rule One Investing](https://www.ruleoneinvesting.com/blog/stock-market-basics/calculate-the-buy-price-of-a-stock/)). Today's price is near that $135 fair value, so the screen says NVDA is roughly fairly priced, not cheap. Reaching $67.72 would take a further drop of about 54% from $145.98. The method is extremely sensitive to the growth rate assumed. The research notes show a purely illustrative Rule #1 sum using 25% growth and a P/E of 50 that lands near $1,000. The inputs your app uses are not in these notes, so the reason for the gap cannot be checked here. The lesson is that a Rule #1 price is only as good as its growth input.

**Why the spread is so wide.** Three choices drive it. First, is FY27 a profit peak or a new base? Second, how fast does growth fade, and is it capped? Third, does the model look backward or forward? Backward-looking methods (Graham Number, projected cash flow) are dragged down by NVDA's small pre-AI history and say very little about it. GF Value is pushed up by the high multiples of the boom years. Forward DCFs land between $170 and $310, depending on how quickly they let growth slow.

## Cyclical rules point to inventory, not price, as the buy signal

Peter Lynch's rule for cyclical stocks turns normal P/E logic upside down. A **low P/E after years of record profits is a warning**, because profits are about to fall. A high P/E, when profits have already collapsed, can mark the bottom ([GuruFocus on Lynch](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/1863600/peter-lynch-how-to-invest-in-cyclical-stocks)). He also watched inventory: stock piling up faster than sales is a red flag ([MoneyWorks4Me](https://www.moneyworks4me.com/blog/peter-lynch-approach-of-investing-in-cyclical-stocks)). These Lynch rules come from secondary summaries, not his books directly. Applied to NVDA today, that rule reads the bulls' best argument, about 9 times FY28 earnings, as the trap.

**NVIDIA's own history supports the inventory rule.** In late 2018 the trigger was excess crypto-related channel inventory; the stock fell about 49% from Oct 1 to mid-December ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/12/12/nvidia-perfect-storm/)). In 2022 it was a $1.32B charge "primarily for inventory and related reserves" as gaming sales fell, and the stock was down more than 57% by late August ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/828754/nvidia-preannounces-lower-revenue-as-pc-gaming-demand-falls.html); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/nvidia-stock-is-down-57-should-you-buy-right-now)). Both times the bottom came while the news was still bad, and the stock got back to its old high about 18 months after the fall began ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2025/03/07/nvidia-stock-down-27-from-peak-history-says-this)). As a reference point only, not a forecast: a 50-57% fall from the $239 record close would land between about **$103 and $120**. NVDA is already down about 40%.

**The current warning lights are on, but demand has not cracked.** Inventory rose from **$21.4B in January to $39.7B in October**, up about 86% in nine months ([NVIDIA press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). Revenue also rose fast, so the ratio is not yet alarming. Supply commitments, mostly multi-year memory purchases at boom-time memory prices, reportedly rose to **$390B** from $279B a quarter earlier ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-nvidias-supply-commitments-hit-390b-7-3x-its-cash-pile/)); one outlet printed $309B, likely a typo. Gross margin is guided down about 3 points. On the other side, hyperscaler (the giant cloud companies) spending is still rising: Alphabet raised its 2026 capital spending to $195-205B and said it will "increase significantly in 2027." Microsoft trimmed its 2026 figure to about $170B, but blamed delays at outside data-center suppliers, not weaker demand ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/magnificent-7-earnings-rush-reveals-ai-spending-surge-with-hyperscaler-capex-set-to-reach-725-billion-in-2026-224901707.html)).

**Strategists disagree on the cycle's stage, not its existence.** Even the optimists forecast sharp slowing. UBS sees hyperscaler spending growth falling from 76% in 2026 to 25% in 2027 and 6% in 2028 ([Express Tribune/Reuters](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2618784/among-ai-crowd-some-investors-position-for-slower-hyperscaler-spending-growth)). Allianz Trade expects 51%, then 13%, then about 5%, and calls it "natural digestion" ([Allianz Trade](https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_global/news-insights/economic-insights/AI-capex-cycle-war-proof-now.html)). Natixis says it is "a moderation in growth" onto a high plateau, not a peak ([Natixis IM](https://www.im.natixis.com/en-us/insights/macro-investing-insights/chart-of-the-week-archives/has-the-semiconductor-cycle-changed)). ## What this means (not advice)

The outside views agree on one thing: on forward earnings, NVDA is the cheapest it has been in years. Every Wall Street target, every current forward DCF and even Morningstar's earlier bear case sits above $146. They disagree on whether that cheapness is real, and that is a question about the cycle and the leadership gap, not about the arithmetic. Analysts kept their earnings forecasts and cut the price they will pay. That means the next moves depend on two things their models do not yet price: who becomes CEO, and whether inventory and memory commitments turn into write-downs if AI spending growth slows in 2027. Your app's two screens are the conservative bookends. Rule #1 says today's price is roughly fair, with no margin of safety. The Lynch screen says a cyclical should be judged on the inventory turn, not on a price.
