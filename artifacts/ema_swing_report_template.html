Do the 21 and 50 EMA setups beat buying on a random day?

Every setup was traded on the watchlist stocks since 2005. Rules were judged on 2005–2018 and then checked on 2019–2026, which nothing was tuned on. A setup gets YES only if it won at least 2 points more often than a random day and had a higher middle result, in both periods.

How to read this, and what it can't tell you

  • Win % is the share of trades that made money. Median is the middle trade. Worst 10% is the result that only one trade in ten did worse than.
  • Each cell shows 2005–18 / 2019–26. The random-day row (gold) is the bar to beat.
  • Plain means sell after the hold. With stop/target means sell on a daily close under the 50 EMA, or at 2× the risk above entry, or at the end of the hold. The YES/NO verdict uses the plain hold so setup and random day are measured the same way.
  • Biggest dip is the median worst point during a trade (stock price); this stands in for drawdown.
  • Calls are modelled with Black-Scholes, not real quotes: 45 days to expiry (60 for the 40-day hold), volatility from the last 60 days held fixed, bought at the ask and sold at the bid (about 8% round trip). Real spreads on small caps are often wider.
  • Only tickers still listed today are included, because Yahoo has no data for delisted names. That makes every row, the random day included, look a little better than reality.
  • Trades on different stocks on the same days move together, so a few points of win % can still be luck. That is why a setup needs to pass in both periods.
